What it is

sovereign-swap settles a trade directly between you and a counterparty on two chains at once, with no exchange and no intermediary holding the funds. Each side locks its coin behind the same hashlock; claiming one side reveals the secret that claims the other. There is no step where anyone but you can move your coin, and every swap ends one of two ways: claimed, or refunded back to you.

  • Your spending keys never leave your node. The relay only carries messages; it holds no funds.
  • No account, no sign-up, no KYC. Offers are public; identities are not.
  • Reached over Tor by default, behind your own password.

BTC and xBTC are different assets

Bitcoin Blake2b (xBTC) is a hardfork of Bitcoin with a different proof of work. It shares Bitcoin's address format, so an address alone never tells you which chain it belongs to. The two coins are not interchangeable. A swap is exactly the tool for crossing between them, deliberately and atomically, instead of sending a coin to an address on the wrong chain by mistake.

Beta: read this before you swap

You must keep your client online for the life of a swap. Once a leg is locked, your node is what claims it, or refunds it before the timelock expires. If your client is offline across the whole refund window while blocks pass, you risk the timelock lapsing. Stay online from the moment you take or publish an offer until the swap shows claimed or refunded.

  • Test and dust amounts only. This is early software. The client caps the BTC side of a swap to a small beta limit; do not put meaningful value through it yet.
  • Run your own nodes. You connect the client to your own Bitcoin Core (BTC) and Bitcoin Knots BLAKE2b (xBTC) nodes. There is no hosted backend.
  • A voluntary fee is on by default and you can turn it off in the client's config. It funds the project; swaps work the same with it off.
  • Report what breaks. Issues, logs and odd behaviour are the point of a beta.

Start here

  1. Download, verify and sideload the client (GPG + checksum).
  2. Expose your nodes' RPC and point the client at them.
  3. Do your first swap, and learn where to report issues.
